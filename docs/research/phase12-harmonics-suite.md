# Phase 12 Harmonics suite

The product target is warmth, movement and useful excess: subtle colour through the middle of a dial, then deliberately extreme breakup at its limit. This is an original instrument/effects design informed by published behaviour, not a reverse-engineered commercial circuit model. The standalone core must remain independent of the browser and suitable for a future plugin wrapper.

## Product and signal ownership

Character owns per-voice identity: note-dependent drift, pitch settling, velocity response, source texture and body. Harmonics owns the summed stereo sound: amplification, saturation and dynamics. A Character shortcut may control the same Suite setting, but cannot instantiate another hidden drive. Fullness retains its independently tested body path this pass so new colour can be assessed without changing two similar processors simultaneously.

The processing contract is a shared stereo suite after voice amplitude/panning and FX-routed Sub, before Width/Delay. Clean Sub bypasses it. Per-voice Harmonics allocations and generic Drive/Harmonics Sauce ownership are removed. Compressor, Colour, Crush and multiband have individual bypass; their visible order matches DSP. Details expose meaningful timing and gain controls. Current format only, strict atomic import, no migrations.

Dynamics is an audio-domain processor. The user explicitly rejected changing ADSR to obtain Tight/Bloom: native note envelopes and voice lifetime therefore remain unchanged. A stereo-linked transient/body detector drives compression and expansion: Tight preserves an onset while containing its body/falling tail; Bloom reduces onset contrast and lifts existing quieter body/tail audio. Silence remains silence. The same processor can act on external audio in a future effects wrapper; it does not rely on MIDI-envelope access. The dry path retains the original source and the declared24sample alignment.

Two serial Colour slots share one oversampling domain and can be independently enabled, styled, blended and trimmed. The expanded palette is Amp, Clip, Sine Crush, Tape and Rectify; each must have measured distinct behaviour. Compressor automatic makeup starts enabled with explicit manual trim. Every processing stage has its own output level, alongside global output. Multiband exposes Low/Mid/High settings rather than a single hidden macro. The final native header is the authoritative byte layout; current-only state may change before this phase is checkpointed.

## Original DSP requirements

Actual transient-shaper source was inspected as well as manuals: [Gin/MGA TranShaper](https://github.com/FigBug/Gin/blob/master/modules/gin_dsp/dsp/gin_transientshaper.h) uses fast/baseline/slow follower ratios for level-relative attack/tail gain, with band-limited processing and bounded smoothing; [Airwindows Point](https://github.com/airwindows/airwindows/blob/master/plugins/WinVST/Point/PointProc.cpp) demonstrates reaction-speed-dependent follower ratios. [SPL's technical explanation, pp7–9](https://spl.audio/wp-content/uploads/TransientDesigner4_9842_OM_E.pdf) describes comparing follower shapes to control audio gain, independently of a MIDI gate. These are useful implementable principles. Our Dynamics remains an original linked processor; none establishes that its timing equals Drum Buss or Softube's private algorithms.

The OTT correction uses a much more concrete reference than a generic three-band compressor: David Braun's explicitly MIT-licensed `xfer_ott` function in [Faust compressors.lib at b8f0e061808aa320b899e82f81043164ace1d00f](https://github.com/grame-cncm/faustlibraries/blob/b8f0e061808aa320b899e82f81043164ace1d00f/compressors.lib). Its measured model combines two soft knees per band, depth-dependent makeup, bounded upward gain and frequency-dependent timing. Our processor adapts these laws with attribution, while retaining its own exact-reconstruction subtractive crossover and quiet-floor protection. Its published error figures do not apply to this modified processor.

For amplifier behaviour, [Softube's Vintage Suite manual](https://www.softube.com/user-manuals/amp-room-vintage-suite) distinguishes preamp and power-amp drive and explains tone-stack interaction with breakup. That reinforces Radiator's staged-amp lesson. Two serial warm-Amp slots provide separately driven stages in our instrument; there is no cabinet/microphone/room model in this phase. [FabFilter Saturn's palette](https://www.fabfilter.com/downloads/pdf/help/ffsaturn2-manual.pdf) is the product reference for distinct tape/amp/rectification colours and per-stage level control. Tape here is a creative memory/bandwidth colour, not a full magnetic-tape transport simulation.

The implementation research extends below product controls. [Chowdhury's virtual-analog comparison](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2009.02833) derives a linear tone network via nodal analysis/bilinear mapping, uses wave-digital networks for component interactions, and compares a trained recurrent model for a nonlinear stage. Its corresponding [KlonCentaur source](https://github.com/jatinchowdhury18/KlonCentaur) is a reference for partitioning a circuit into useful modelling blocks. Our choice is explicit staged DSP for predictable editing and standalone integration; a trained model would need target-circuit data and parameter coverage that we do not currently possess.

Actual open-code inspection: [BYOD TubeProc](https://github.com/Chowdhury-DSP/BYOD/blob/main/src/processors/drive/tube_amp/TubeProc.h) contains a wave-digital capacitor network with a voltage-dependent current source and retained plate voltage; [TubeAmp](https://github.com/Chowdhury-DSP/BYOD/blob/main/src/processors/drive/tube_amp/TubeAmp.cpp) wraps it with smoothed input/output gain and startup settling. The [Compressor source](https://github.com/Chowdhury-DSP/BYOD/blob/main/src/processors/other/Compressor.cpp) separates level detection, a soft-knee gain computer and linked channel multiplication. These are primary implementation clues, not evidence of Soundtoys internals. No GPL source was copied or added as a dependency.

[Holters' stateful antialiasing paper](https://www.dafx.de/paper-archive/2019/DAFx2019_paper_4.pdf) shows why applying antiderivative processing inside stateful systems requires careful treatment of delay and frequency response. Even identity ADAA contributes half-sample delay/lowpass response. The new suite therefore uses a declared integer-latency architecture, measures wet/dry reconstruction, and does not simply drop the old processor into a new feedback path. This paper supplies design cautions, not a claim that our Amp solves an Altec circuit.

Use stereo-linked envelope detection so gain changes do not pull the stereo image. A conventional soft-knee downward compressor should have threshold/ratio/attack/release controls. Its static dB curve above threshold T follows y=T+(x−T)/R, interpolated through the knee; smoothing belongs to detector/gain state rather than arbitrary UI animation. The Crush path should provide much stronger pressure and audible memory, not duplicate a conventional compressor at a different threshold.

Colour should have meaningful low-to-high drive progression, explicit output trim, stable DC behaviour and aligned wet/dry. Memoryful multistage amplification can distinguish warm Amp from hard Clip and periodic Sine Crush. Oversampling/antialiasing quality must be measured on sine and broadband sources at low and high pitches; no alias-free claim follows merely from oversampling.

Multiband needs complementary reconstruction, bounded upward lift, a quiet floor, independent up/down strength and time scaling. Depth0 and bypass must avoid unexpected crossover coloration. Verify summed unity separately from mono compatibility and time-domain dry/wet alignment: a magnitude-flat crossover can still alter phase. Do not disguise this with peak normalization or independently normalizing each comparison clip.

## Acceptance gates

- Neutral, disabled, Mix0 and all-stage bypass: exact or explicitly measured latency-aligned agreement; no note duration corruption.
- Dynamics: measurable attack/body contrast and recovery on real audio plucks, held notes and external-style bursts at Tight/Neutral/Bloom; ADSR settings and source voice completion remain unchanged. Mix0 returns aligned original audio; no claim of regenerating dead source tails.
- Compressor/Crush: measured static gain, attack, release and recovery; actual pumping/containment at extreme settings, finite silence/noise behaviour.
- Colour: normalized spectral contrast, Drive/Tone sweeps, high-note alias limits, DC and peak bounds, real input/output versus separately labelled transfer previews.
- Multiband: crossover sum, per-band gain activity, upward noise bound, stereo linking, low-rate/sample-rate variation and depth transitions.
- Real-time: one global processor, allocation-free render, stable clock/note ownership, representative polyphonic WASM timings rather than isolated-stage claims.
- UX: independent review can reject layout/interaction/feedback; desktop and phone widths, full dial reset, Undo/import, stage bypass and truthful visual feedback.
- Listening: fixed-gain and loudness-matched auditions remain separate. Automated measurements do not establish that the result matches Soundtoys/Softube or earns the user's subjective approval.
